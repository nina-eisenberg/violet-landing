1. Make the conversational editing target unmistakable. HIGH
Observed: opening Summary sets the composer to “Editing Marcus Doyle's summary”, but the composer is behind the modal. The main placeholder remains the generic case prompt.
Recommendation: put “Ask Violet to change this summary” inside the artifact view, or provide a visible attached composer. Keep a named target above it and a clear exit. Verify target clears appropriately on close.
Joy: the investigator can immediately ask for a change without wondering where the words will go.

2. Make citations comfortably discoverable and accessible. HIGH
Observed: source numbers are visually tiny superscripts. They are visible in the screenshot but were not exposed as buttons in the summary's accessibility snapshot. The instruction says to click a marker.
Recommendation: retain restrained superscripts but give each a generous target, keyboard focus, and accessible label naming the source. Hover/focus should highlight the supported sentence. Opening a source should preserve reading position.
Limit: keyboard activation and actual source-opening behavior not yet tested in this pass.

3. Clarify saved versus final in the plan. MEDIUM
Observed: shelf says “Final · 4 allegations”; the plan modal allows immediate editing and says “it saves as you go.”
Recommendation: show the plan's current final/draft status inside the modal; after an edit, explicitly show whether finalization was invalidated. One small “Saved” acknowledgement near the changed field is enough.
Limit: did not edit the existing finalized plan, so not claiming a persistence defect.

4. Reduce duplicate identity and hierarchy noise. LOW
Observed: person window title “Marcus Doyle · Details”, subtitle “Marcus Doyle”, selected Summary tab. “Bottom line” is inline bold while “Background” is a section heading.
Recommendation: person name once, role underneath, active tab conveys the task. Give Bottom line the same section hierarchy as the other headings, or style it as a clear lead summary rather than an undersized pseudo-heading.
Joy: calmer, easier scanning, less interface to decode.

5. Distinguish same-named cases at selection time. MEDIUM
Observed: three open Marcus Doyle cases in Home navigation, differentiated primarily by case number. This fixture makes ambiguity unusually visible.
Recommendation: disambiguation cards should include case number, stage, organization/context and last activity. A short “the one with the completed interviews” reply should resolve the offered set.
Demo: preserve case-selection state; do not jump immediately to a report from a generic name.

6. Treat confirmation as the interaction, not a second explanation. PRESERVE
Current prompt explicitly requires cards to speak for themselves; no “the card is up”; show a finished draft immediately; one confirmation, not two; short milestone acknowledgement without confetti.
Recommendation: preserve this in the demo. Do not revive old narration from stored conversations.
Joy: responsiveness comes from the work appearing, not Violet announcing that she will show it.

7. Protect the thread from old-state confusion. VERIFY NEXT
Historical thread contains duplicate task warnings and old support results; current shelf has one open task. Do not classify these as current regressions.
Recommendation to investigate: visibly retire obsolete proposals; make pending items and the latest artifact easy to find. Keep the milestone jump and shelf.

8. Voice must respect actual confirmation boundaries. DEMO REQUIREMENT
Current main allows spoken yes for tasks, notes, scheduling and interview status. Reporter replies require “send it”; outlines, summaries, new cases and other document/external writes require a tap. The proposed demo must not pretend every action can complete by voice alone.
Recommendation: when a tap is required, show the relevant card and explain the boundary once, concisely. Avoid repeating it on every turn.

Observed: the timeline presents all 20 substantial events; the interview summary is also long. Preserve the full record but start with key events and expandable detail, clearly showing counts and filters. For the marketing demo, use selected excerpts labelled as such.

11. The report has a real workspace, not another chat card (keep).
Observed: Report opens alongside the conversation, with Draft and Review & finalize. The loaded report contains all eight sections. Review has highlighted/flag context and a source area; this case currently has no open flags. Keep the complete report visible when showing a revision or zoomed review. Do not imply our illustrative flag is an outstanding flag in the actual case.

12. Celebrate genuine completion (keep).
Current main distinguishes a checked report from a finalized report. Celebration belongs after review and finalization, not after generation. This restraint makes the product feel trustworthy.
