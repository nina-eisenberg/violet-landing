• Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Religious Accommodation Policy?

    Evidence aligned

    Your finding: Substantiated

    Evidence suggests: Substantiated (high confidence)

    Assessment limitations

    • No Jordan Pierce interview was provided, so the regional-level authority and swap rules are known only from the August 24 email and Doyle's account.
    • No HR consultation records were supplied for review, so the finding that Doyle did not consult HR rests on his interview and the absence of any such record in the supplied materials.
    • The record does not address whether undue hardship would have applied to the accommodation, and the finding does not depend on that.
    Evidence breakdown

    Supporting factors

    • Scope question 1 (written response timing): Goldberg emailed on September 4 and Doyle replied in writing on September 14 at 5:48 PM. The app's day count puts that on business day 5, within the window, on its last day. HR-114 item 2 requires a response "within five (5) business days", so that element is not shown to be breached.
    • Scope question 2 (alternatives): HR-114 item 3 requires that "Before denying a request, the supervisor must consider reasonable alternatives, including voluntary shift swaps... and must discuss them with the employee." Doyle admits he saw Kim's September 10 swap offer and "didn't pursue it", and the September 14 denial email makes no mention of alternatives. Goldberg says he never asked her about options, and Doyle did not claim any discussion with her.
    • Doyle's stated reason for not pursuing the swap, that he thought swaps were not allowed, is contradicted by Pierce's August 24 email. That email says supervisors "may approve individual shift swaps between reps with the same skills". Goldberg's own September 4 request also offered to take weekend mornings or another shift.
    • Scope question 2 (HR consultation): HR-114 item 4 requires that a supervisor who believes a request cannot be granted "must consult HR before denying it." Nothing in the record shows Doyle consulted HR, and he did not say he did; his interview describes only his belief that he lacked authority. HR first received the matter through Goldberg's September 15 complaint, after the September 14 denial.

    Counter-evidence

    • The written response was sent on business day 5, so the timing portion of the policy is not breached.
    • Doyle says he sincerely believed he lacked authority over a regionally set rotation, which bears on intent but not on the item 3 and 4 process duties.
    • The record contains no HR consultation log or Doyle statement on that point beyond his interview, so the absence of HR consultation rests on the supplied record rather than a direct denial.

  • Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Anti-Harassment Policy?

    Evidence aligned

    Your finding: Not Substantiated

    Evidence suggests: Not Substantiated (high confidence)

    Assessment limitations

    • The "find a job that fits her religion" remark rests on Kim's secondhand account of what Carla said; Carla's interview notes deny it, and no direct witness was identified.
    • The date and context of the alleged "find a job" remark are not given in the record.
    • Carla Rivera's interview is summarized in notes rather than a transcript.
    • No evidence of other religion-related conduct by Doyle beyond these remarks was supplied.
    Evidence breakdown

    Supporting factors

    • Conduct is partly established: Doyle admits he 'probably' said "We'll see about Fridays" and 'might have said something like' the "holy day" remark, and Kim heard both firsthand. The "find a job that fits her religion" remark is not established, since Kim only heard it secondhand from Carla, Carla denies saying it, and Doyle denies it.
    • The policy requires conduct "based on a protected characteristic"; the "holy day" remark has a plausible religious reference, but "We'll see about Fridays" is facially neutral and Doyle's explanation (rotation outcome) is plausible.
    • The policy requires conduct "sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment"; two brief remarks over one or two days, with no slur, epithet, threat or name-calling, are not shown to be severe or pervasive.
    • The policy alternatively requires conduct that "affects the terms and conditions of employment"; the remarks themselves did not change terms, and the record ties the Friday denial to the regional rotation rather than to the remarks (that denial is addressed under the Religious Accommodation Policy).

    Counter-evidence

    • The remarks came from the supervisor who was handling Goldberg's religious accommodation request, and the "holy day" comment, made right after the huddle, can be read as dismissive of religious observance.
    • The Friday denial did affect Goldberg's schedule, and Goldberg says Doyle looked at her when he made the "We'll see about Fridays" remark; Kim corroborates the remark but not the look or the intent.
    • Kim's account of the "holy day" remark is firsthand and partly admitted by Doyle, so the remark is probably established.

  • Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Anti-Retaliation Policy?

    Evidence aligned

    Your finding: Not Substantiated

    Evidence suggests: Not Substantiated (high confidence)

    Assessment limitations

    • The September 10 email from Rivera to supervisors was described by Rivera but not supplied as a document.
    • Rivera's account is from interview notes by Anand, not a transcript.
    • No evidence was supplied on whether or how Doyle could have learned of the complaint before September 17, such as whether HR contacted him earlier; his account of when he learned is uncorroborated beyond Rivera's lack of knowledge.
    • Not Substantiated means the evidence does not establish that the removal was because of protected activity; it does not prove the removal was unconnected to it.
    Evidence breakdown

    Supporting factors

    • Conduct is established: Doyle removed Goldberg from the escalations queue ($1.75/hour differential) on September 16, and the WFM log records this; removing a pay differential could qualify as an adverse action (reduction in pay), so that element is likely met.
    • The causation element ("because they engaged in protected activity") is not shown: Doyle removed four reps (Goldberg, Okafor, Tran, Bell) within about two minutes, each logged with the same reason, "Cert not on file (LMS pull 9/10)", so Goldberg was not singled out.
    • Rivera, who is outside Doyle's reporting line, corroborates that she emailed the uncertified list to all supervisors on September 10, that the certification became mandatory September 15, and that the list was wrong for Goldberg and Tran because of the weekly Friday vendor sync lag; the LMS export shows Goldberg's August 28 completion first appeared September 11, after the pull.
    • Doyle states he first learned of the HR complaint on September 17, the day after the removal; nothing in the record contradicts this, and Rivera also said she did not know of the complaint, which undercuts retaliatory knowledge/motive.

    Counter-evidence

    • Timing is close: the removal occurred the day after the September 15 complaint and about two days after Doyle denied the accommodation request, and Anti-Retaliation Policy 4.4 says timing is considered.
    • Goldberg had actually completed the certification on August 28, so the removal was in error, and Doyle apparently did not verify the list before acting; Goldberg also believes she was the only one removed, though the log shows otherwise.
    • Policy 5.3 expects managers to consult HR and document reasons for decisions affecting employees who recently engaged in protected activity, and there is no record he did so for the removal; this goes to process, not to retaliatory motive.
