I. Introduction

On September 15, 2026, Leah Goldberg, Member Services Representative II in the Contact Center, filed a formal complaint with HR Business Partner Priya Anand concerning her supervisor, Marcus Doyle, Team Supervisor, Member Services. Goldberg alleged that Doyle denied her request for a religious accommodation to avoid Friday evening shifts, failed to discuss alternatives despite a coworker's offer to swap shifts, made remarks about religion in connection with the Q4 rotation, and removed her from the escalations queue the day after she complained to HR.

This investigation examined three questions: whether Doyle's handling of the accommodation request violated the Religious Accommodation Policy, HR-114; whether his remarks violated the Anti-Harassment Policy; and whether his removal of Goldberg from escalations violated the Anti-Retaliation Policy. Goldberg's request that HR secure her Friday evenings free from work is a remedy for the organization to decide and was not investigated.

II. Summary of Findings

Religious Accommodation Policy. Marcus Doyle denied Leah Goldberg's request for a religious accommodation without considering and discussing reasonable alternatives with her, including the voluntary shift swap Jordan Kim offered in the team chat on September 10 (Exhibit 2). Doyle confirmed that he saw the offer and did not pursue it, stating he believed swaps were not allowed during the Q4 rollout. His written denial on September 14 addressed neither the swap nor the alternative shifts Goldberg had proposed, and Goldberg stated he never asked her about other options. The August 24 email from Regional Operations Manager Jordan Pierce explicitly authorized supervisors to approve shift swaps between representatives with the same skills (Exhibit 2). The response was timely under Policy HR-114's five-business-day requirement, which excludes weekends and the September 7 company holiday.¹ This allegation is substantiated.

Anti-Harassment Policy. The evidence establishes that Doyle made two remarks connected to the Friday rotation: "We'll see about Fridays" at the September 9 huddle, and a remark after the huddle that "If everyone wanted their holy day off, nobody would work Fridays" (Exhibits 1 and 2). The third remark attributed to him—that Goldberg should find a job that fits her religion—is not established; Carla Rivera, the claimed source, denied hearing it and said she told Jordan Kim only that Doyle had been "grumbling about the Friday requests"¹³³ (Exhibit 3). The two established remarks do not rise to conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment under the Anti-Harassment Policy. The first remark was ambiguous and not directed at Goldberg; the second was a single general comment about staffing that Goldberg learned of only secondhand. This allegation is not substantiated.

Anti-Retaliation Policy. Doyle removed Goldberg from the escalations queue on September 16, the morning after her HR complaint, citing an uncertified status based on a Workforce Management list pulled on September 10, before the complaint (Exhibit 3). However, Doyle removed three other representatives—Ngozi Okafor, Kevin Tran, and Amy Bell—on the same list and with the identical reason, within three minutes (Exhibit 3). Kevin Tran's certification, like Goldberg's, first appeared in the LMS on September 11, five days before removal, indicating the list was stale. Carla Rivera re-pulled the list on September 21 and restored both Goldberg and Tran together, recorded as "Cert confirmed" (Exhibit 3). The contemporaneous change log and the uniform treatment of four representatives corroborate Doyle's account that he applied a pre-existing list uniformly and did not single out Goldberg. This allegation is not substantiated.

III. Investigative Methodology

This investigation applied the preponderance of the evidence standard: a fact is established where the evidence on one side outweighs the evidence on the other side, assessed qualitatively rather than by count of witnesses.

This Investigator interviewed four people: Leah Goldberg, Complainant, Member Services Representative II, on September 17, 2026, documented by transcript; Jordan Kim, Witness, Member Services Representative, on September 17, 2026, documented by transcript; Marcus Doyle, Subject, Team Supervisor, Member Services, on September 18, 2026, documented by transcript; and Carla Rivera, Witness, on September 18, 2026, documented by notes. Jordan Pierce, Regional Operations Manager, who set the Q4 rotation and was named in the case record, was not interviewed.

This Investigator reviewed four exhibits: the complaint email from Leah Goldberg to HR Business Partner Priya Anand dated September 15, 2026 (Exhibit 1); email and team chat records including the Q4 rotation announcement, Leah's accommodation request, Jordan Kim's shift swap offer, and Marcus Doyle's denial (Exhibit 2); Workforce Management and training records showing escalations queue changes and LMS certification data (Exhibit 3); and the Religious Accommodation Policy, HR-114, effective January 1, 2026 (Exhibit 4).

IV. Background

On September 15, 2026, at 8:12 AM, Leah Goldberg, Member Services Representative II in the Contact Center, emailed a formal complaint to HR Business Partner Priya Anand concerning her supervisor, Marcus Doyle, Team Supervisor, Member Services (Exhibit 1).² Leah stated that she would reach four years of tenure in November and that Marcus became her supervisor in February 2026, and Marcus confirmed that he has had the team since February.³

In her complaint, Leah alleges three things. First, she alleges that she asked Marcus on September 4 not to be scheduled on Friday evenings past 5 PM because she observes Shabbat.⁴ Second, she alleges that Marcus "never talked to me about it"¹³⁴ despite a coworker's offer to swap Fridays, and that he "took ten days to answer and then just said no."⁵ Third, she alleges that he made remarks at and after a team huddle on September 9 that she connected to her religious observance (Exhibit 1).⁶ She wrote that she does not feel her religion is respected on the team, and she asked HR to help her keep her Friday evenings free.⁷

In her interview on September 17, 2026, Leah further alleged that she was removed from the escalations queue, which carries a pay differential, on September 16, the day after she went to HR.⁸ Jordan Kim, Member Services Representative, who sits next to Leah, reported hearing from Carla Rivera that Marcus had said Leah should find a job that fits her religion.⁹ Carla Rivera is a Workforce Management Analyst in the Contact Center who supports scheduling and queue assignments for Member Services and is not in Marcus's reporting line.¹⁰

The investigation set out to determine three questions. The first is whether Marcus's handling of Leah's request complied with the Religious Accommodation Policy, HR-114 (Exhibit 4).¹¹ This includes whether he responded in writing within five business days, whether he considered and discussed reasonable alternatives with her, including the shift swap Jordan offered, and whether he consulted HR before denying the request.¹² The second is whether Marcus made remarks about religion, or about Leah's religious observance, in connection with the Q4 Friday rotation that bear on the Anti-Harassment Policy. The third is whether Leah's removal from the escalations queue on September 16 bears on the Anti-Retaliation Policy.

Leah's request that HR secure her Friday evenings free from work is a remedy for the organization to decide, not a question of fact, and was not investigated in this report.

V. Facts

The Q4 Rotation and the Accommodation Request

On Monday, August 24, 2026, Jordan Pierce, Regional Operations Manager, emailed Contact Center supervisors that the Q4 rotation was approved and that every Member Services Representative would work one Friday 2–10 PM shift per week starting September 18.¹³ The same email, contained in Exhibit 2 (labelled in the original as "Exhibit A"), stated that "Team supervisors may approve individual shift swaps between reps with the same skills"¹³⁶ and that swaps were to be logged with Workforce Management.¹⁴ Leah Goldberg, Member Services Representative II, recalled that the rotation came out on August 31.¹⁵ Jordan Pierce was not interviewed.

On Friday, September 4, 2026, at 10:41 AM, Leah emailed her supervisor, Marcus Doyle, Team Supervisor, Member Services.¹⁶ She explained that she observes Shabbat, which begins at sundown on Fridays, asked to be kept off Friday shifts after 5 PM, and offered to "take weekend mornings or another shift instead."¹⁷ Exhibit 4, Policy HR-114, lists Labor Day, Monday, September 7, as a 2026 company holiday and excludes weekends and company holidays from the five-business-day response period.¹⁸

The Shift Swap Offer and the Denial

On Thursday, September 10, at 9:02 AM, Jordan Kim, Member Services Representative, posted in the team chat, tagging Marcus.¹⁹ He wrote, "I can take Leah's Friday evening shifts if she takes my Saturday mornings."²⁰ At 9:15 AM, Marcus reacted with a thumbs up, as shown in Exhibit 2.²¹ Jordan said that Marcus "never said yes or no" and that nobody followed up with him.²² Marcus confirmed that he saw the offer and explained, "I didn't think swaps were allowed during the Q4 rollout, so I didn't pursue it."²³

On Monday, September 14, at 5:48 PM, Marcus replied to Leah: "No. Not this quarter. The rotation is set and I can't make exceptions for one person."²⁴ Marcus explained that Jordan Pierce set the rotation at the regional level and that he did not think he had the authority to change it.²⁵ Leah said that Marcus never discussed other options with her: "He never asked me anything."²⁶ Marcus was not asked whether he consulted HR before denying the request, and the record contains no evidence on that point.

Remarks About Fridays and Religion

Leah recalled that at the September 9 team huddle, when someone asked about requests, Marcus said "Fine." and then "looked right at me and said 'We'll see about Fridays.'"²⁷ Jordan, who attended the huddle, recalled that Marcus said "something like 'Fine' and then 'we'll see about Fridays,'" and added that he "didn't think much of it then."²⁸ Jordan did not describe where Marcus was looking.²⁹ Marcus said he "Probably" made the remark, but he explained that he meant they would see how the rotation worked out and that "It wasn't aimed at Leah."³⁰

Jordan said that after the huddle Marcus was at his desk "talking to himself, kind of loud."³¹ Jordan recalled hearing Marcus say, "If everyone wanted their holy day off, nobody would work Fridays."³² Jordan said he heard this himself from about six feet away.³³ Leah did not hear the remark; she said that Jordan told her about it afterwards, and her complaint places that conversation later the same day.³⁴³⁵ Marcus said, "I might have said something like that," and explained that the remark concerned staffing in general, not Leah.³⁶

Jordan also said that Carla Rivera, Workforce Management Analyst, Contact Center, told him that Marcus had said "something like Leah should find a job that fits her religion."³⁷ Jordan said he did not hear that remark himself.³⁸ Carla said she never heard Marcus say that Leah should find another job.³⁹ She also said she did not hear him say anything about Leah's religion, and that she told Jordan only that Marcus had been "grumbling about the Friday requests."⁴⁰ Marcus denied the remark: "No. I never said that."⁴¹ No source gives a date for this remark.

The Complaint and the Escalations Queue

Carla said that the escalations certification became mandatory on September 15 at the direction of regional operations, and Marcus gave the same start date.⁴²¹²¹ Carla said that on September 10 she pulled from the LMS a list of representatives without a completed certification and emailed it to all team supervisors.⁴³ She said the list included four representatives on Marcus's team: Leah, Ngozi Okafor, Kevin Tran and Amy Bell.⁴⁴ That email is not in the record. According to the LMS export in Exhibit 3, Leah completed the course on August 28 and the completion first appeared in the LMS on September 11.⁴⁵ Kevin completed the course and it appeared on September 11, while Ngozi and Amy had not completed it.⁴⁶ Carla explained that certifications appear in the LMS only after a weekly Friday vendor sync, so a completion late in the week can take up to a week to show.⁴⁷ Leah said she has a completion email, which is not in the record.⁴⁸

Leah emailed her formal complaint to HR Business Partner Priya Anand on Tuesday, September 15, at 8:12 AM, as shown in Exhibit 1.⁴⁹ The Workforce Management change log in Exhibit 3 shows that on September 16, between 7:05 and 7:07 AM, "M. Doyle" removed Leah, Ngozi, Kevin and Amy from escalations, each with the reason "Cert not on file (LMS pull 9/10)."⁵⁰ The escalations queue carries a differential of $1.75 per hour.⁵¹ Leah recalled the differential as about two dollars an hour.⁵² Leah said that "As far as I know I was the only one taken off"¹⁴⁴ and that she believes the removal was connected to her complaint.⁵³ Jordan said he heard that "a few people got taken off, not just Leah."⁵⁴ Marcus said he removed everyone on Carla's list and "didn't pick anyone."⁵⁵ He also said he first learned of the complaint when Priya Anand called him on September 17.⁵⁶ Carla said she did not know Leah had raised any complaint.⁵⁷ No document in the record shows when Marcus learned of the complaint.

On September 21, Carla re-pulled the list and restored Leah at 11:30 AM and Kevin at 11:31 AM, each recorded as "Cert confirmed (LMS re-pull 9/21)."⁵⁸ Leah confirmed that she was put back on escalations that day and said that Carla told her the list was wrong.⁵⁹

VI. Findings: Religious Accommodation Policy

Allegation: Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Religious Accommodation Policy?

There is sufficient evidence to substantiate that, more likely than not, Marcus Doyle, Team Supervisor, Member Services, denied Leah Goldberg's request for a religious accommodation without considering and discussing reasonable alternatives with her, including the voluntary shift swap Jordan Kim offered. There is insufficient evidence to substantiate that, more likely than not, Marcus failed to respond in writing within five business days.

Policy HR-114, Exhibit 4, provides in relevant part: "2. A supervisor who receives a request must respond in writing within five (5) business days. Business days exclude weekends and company holidays. 3. Before denying a request, the supervisor must consider reasonable alternatives, including voluntary shift swaps, schedule changes and transfers, and must discuss them with the employee. 4. A supervisor who believes a request cannot be granted must consult HR before denying it."⁶⁰

Timeliness of the Response

Leah, Member Services Representative II, made her request by email on Friday, September 4, 2026, as shown in Exhibit 2.⁶¹ Policy HR-114 excludes weekends and company holidays from the response period, and it lists Labor Day, Monday, September 7, as a company holiday.⁶² Counting from September 4, the five business days are September 8, 9, 10, 11 and 14. Marcus replied in writing on Monday, September 14, at 5:48 PM, as shown in Exhibit 2.⁶³ Leah's complaint that the reply "took ten days" is accurate as a count of calendar days.⁶⁴ However, it does not account for the weekends and holiday that the policy excludes. Her account on this point is sincere, but the documents do not bear out a breach of paragraph 2.

Consideration and Discussion of Alternatives

The evidence that Marcus neither pursued nor discussed alternatives with Leah comes from several sources that agree. First, Marcus himself confirmed that he saw Jordan's swap offer and "didn't pursue it."⁶⁵ Second, Jordan Kim, Member Services Representative, said that Marcus reacted with a thumbs up but "never said yes or no," and that nobody followed up with him.⁶⁶ The chat record in Exhibit 2 shows the thumbs-up reaction and no further reply.⁶⁷ Third, Marcus's written denial addresses neither the swap nor the alternative shifts Leah offered in her request.⁶⁸ Fourth, Leah said Marcus "never asked me anything."⁶⁹ Leah's own statement is not independent corroboration of itself. However, Marcus's admission and the documents confirm it, and Marcus did not claim to have discussed any option with her.

The strongest evidence against this conclusion is Marcus's explanation. He said that Jordan Pierce set the rotation at the regional level, that he did not think he had the authority to change it, and that he did not think swaps were allowed during the Q4 rollout.⁷⁰ He added, "I thought my hands were tied."⁷¹ Nothing in the record suggests that this belief was insincere. It is nonetheless contrary to the August 24 email from Jordan Pierce, Regional Operations Manager, in Exhibit 2, which states that "Team supervisors may approve individual shift swaps between reps with the same skills."⁷² Moreover, paragraph 3 requires that alternatives be considered and discussed with the employee before a denial, whatever the supervisor concludes about them.⁷³ A sincere but mistaken belief that swaps were unavailable therefore explains the omission, but it does not supply the consideration and discussion the policy requires. The thumbs-up reaction shows that Marcus acknowledged the offer.⁷⁴ It does not show that he weighed it or raised it with Leah, and Marcus did not say that it meant anything more.

Consultation with HR

Marcus was not asked whether he consulted HR before denying the request, and the record contains no evidence on that point. This Investigator therefore makes no determination that Marcus failed to consult HR under paragraph 4. The finding rests on paragraph 3 alone.

Conclusion

The response was made within the period paragraph 2 allows. The record establishes that Marcus denied the request without considering and discussing reasonable alternatives with Leah, as paragraph 3 requires. Whether that conduct constitutes a violation of Policy HR-114 is a determination for the Investigator. For these reasons, there is sufficient evidence to substantiate that, more likely than not, Marcus denied Leah's accommodation request without considering and discussing reasonable alternatives with her, including the voluntary shift swap Jordan offered.

VII. Findings: Anti-Harassment Policy

Allegation: Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Anti-Harassment Policy?

There is insufficient evidence to substantiate that, more likely than not, Marcus Doyle's conduct violated the Anti-Harassment Policy. The evidence establishes that Marcus made two remarks about Fridays, one of which referred to a "holy day."⁷⁵⁷⁶ The evidence does not establish the third remark attributed to him. In this Investigator's judgment, the remarks that are established do not meet the policy's definition of harassment.

The Anti-Harassment Policy provides that "Harassment includes unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that is sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment, or that affects the terms and conditions of employment."⁷⁷ The policy lists verbal harassment, including "Offensive jokes, slurs, epithets, or name-calling," among the conduct prohibited when based on or related to protected characteristics, and it lists religion as a protected characteristic.⁷⁸

Leah Goldberg, Member Services Representative II, alleges that Marcus, Team Supervisor, Member Services, made three remarks connected to her religious observance and the Q4 Friday rotation.⁷⁹⁸⁰ This Investigator has assessed each remark separately, because evidence about one occasion does not corroborate another.

"We'll see about Fridays"

The evidence establishes that Marcus said "We'll see about Fridays" at the September 9 huddle. Leah recalled the remark, Jordan Kim, Member Services Representative, recalled "something like" it, and Marcus said he "Probably" said it.⁸¹ The parties disagree about whether the remark was directed at Leah. Leah recalled that Marcus "looked right at me" and said that "Everyone knew what he meant."⁸² Contrary to Leah's account, Marcus said the remark meant they would see how the rotation worked out and that "It wasn't aimed at Leah."⁸³ Jordan attended the huddle but did not describe where Marcus was looking, and he said he "didn't think much of it then."⁸⁴ No independent corroboration was available for Leah's account that Marcus looked at her. Leah may sincerely have understood the remark as directed at her, given that her request was pending at the time. However, the words themselves do not refer to religion, and the evidence does not show, more likely than not, that Marcus aimed them at her observance.

"If everyone wanted their holy day off, nobody would work Fridays"

The evidence establishes that, after the huddle, Marcus said words to the effect of "If everyone wanted their holy day off, nobody would work Fridays."⁸⁵ Jordan is the only witness who heard the remark.⁸⁶ He said he heard it himself from about six feet away, while Marcus was at his desk "talking to himself, kind of loud."⁸⁷

Jordan's account has weaknesses that this Investigator has weighed. First, Jordan has a relationship to the outcome. He sits next to Leah, he offered her the shift swap, and he closed his interview by saying "Leah's great."⁸⁸ Second, Jordan discussed the remark with Leah afterwards.⁸⁹⁹⁰ Leah's account of the remark is therefore hearsay and does not independently corroborate it. These weaknesses do not displace Jordan's account, however. Marcus said, "I might have said something like that."⁹¹ That partial admission corroborates the substance of what Jordan heard.

The remark refers to religious observance.⁹² Marcus explained that it concerned staffing in general and not Leah.⁹³ Jordan's account is consistent with that explanation in two respects: he did not describe the remark as addressed to anyone, and he did not say it named Leah.⁹⁴ The remark coincided with Leah's pending request, and it could reasonably be heard as referring to it.⁹⁵⁹⁶ The record shows it was a single remark, not addressed to Leah, and not heard by her.⁹⁷⁹⁸

"Find a job that fits her religion"

The evidence does not establish that Marcus said Leah should find a job that fits her religion. First, the only account of the remark is secondhand at two removes. Jordan said he heard it from Carla Rivera, Workforce Management Analyst, and did not hear it himself.⁹⁹ Second, Carla, the claimed source, said she never heard Marcus say Leah should find another job.¹⁰⁰ She also said she did not hear him say anything about Leah's religion, and that she told Jordan only that Marcus had been "grumbling about the Friday requests." The record shows Carla is not in Marcus's reporting line, and it shows no tie between her and either party that bears on this account.¹⁰² Third, Marcus denied the remark.¹⁰³ Fourth, no source gives a date for it. Jordan may honestly have recounted what he understood Carla to say, but the evidence does not show that the remark was made.

Application of the Policy

As summarized above, the evidence establishes two remarks: an ambiguous remark about Fridays at the huddle, and a single remark referring to a "holy day" that Marcus made at his desk. The strongest evidence against this finding is the "holy day" remark. A supervisor made it about religious observance, on the same day he made the huddle remark, while Leah's accommodation request was pending.¹⁰⁴¹⁰⁵¹⁰⁶

In this Investigator's judgment, that evidence does not change the outcome. The remark was a general comment about Friday staffing, not a joke, slur, epithet or name directed at Leah. It occurred once, and Leah learned of it only secondhand.¹⁰⁷ The record does not show remarks of this kind on other occasions. Whether the remark reflects on Marcus's handling of the accommodation request is addressed under the Religious Accommodation Policy. Taken together, the established remarks do not rise to conduct "sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment,"¹⁵³ and the record does not show that they affected the terms and conditions of Leah's employment.¹⁰⁸

For these reasons, there is not sufficient evidence from which to infer that, more likely than not, Marcus Doyle's conduct violated the Anti-Harassment Policy.

VIII. Findings: Anti-Retaliation Policy

Allegation: Did Marcus Doyle's conduct violate the Anti-Retaliation Policy?

There is insufficient evidence to substantiate that, more likely than not, Marcus Doyle removed Leah Goldberg from the escalations queue because she requested a religious accommodation or because she complained to HR.

The Anti-Retaliation Policy provides: "Retaliation means any adverse action taken against an employee because they engaged in protected activity. Retaliation can be direct or indirect, obvious or subtle."¹⁰⁹ It further provides: "Employees must not take any adverse action against another employee because that employee engaged in protected activity. This prohibition applies regardless of whether the underlying complaint or report was ultimately substantiated."¹¹⁰ Policy HR-114 likewise states: "No employee may be retaliated against for requesting an accommodation or for raising a concern under this policy."¹¹¹

Leah engaged in two forms of activity that the policy lists as protected. She requested an accommodation on September 4, and she complained to HR on September 15 (Exhibits 1 and 2).¹¹²¹¹³ Marcus removed her from escalations, which carries a $1.75 per hour differential, on September 16 (Exhibit 3).¹¹⁴ The question is whether the removal was taken because of either activity.

First, the documentary record shows that Marcus treated Leah the same as three other representatives. The Workforce Management change log shows that he removed Leah, Ngozi Okafor, Kevin Tran and Amy Bell within three minutes, each with the identical reason "Cert not on file (LMS pull 9/10)" (Exhibit 3).¹¹⁵ Nothing in the record shows that the other three representatives had requested an accommodation or complained to HR. This contemporaneous record corroborates Marcus's account that he "removed everyone on the list"¹⁵⁶ and "didn't pick anyone," and it is independent of his account.¹¹⁶ Contrary to Leah's assertion that she was the only one removed, the change log shows four removals.¹¹⁷¹¹⁸ Leah qualified that statement with "As far as I know," and nothing suggests she was not sincere.¹¹⁹ Her belief is not borne out by the documents. Jordan Kim's statement that he heard "a few people got taken off"¹⁵⁷ is secondhand and adds no independent weight.¹²⁰

Second, the reason recorded for the removal rests on a list dated before the complaint. Carla Rivera said she pulled the list of uncertified representatives on September 10 and sent it to all team supervisors, and that the certification became mandatory on September 15. Marcus gave the same start date. The list therefore predates Leah's HR complaint by five days. Carla's September 10 email is not in the record. Her account is consistent with the reason recorded in the change log, however, and with the LMS export, which shows that Leah's completion first appeared on September 11 (Exhibit 3).¹²² Carla is not in Marcus's reporting line.¹²³ She compiled the list that later proved wrong as to Leah and Kevin, which gives her some connection to the outcome.¹²⁴ That connection does not lessen the weight of her account, because the change log and LMS export independently support each material point she gave.

Third, the later correction applied to Leah and Kevin alike. On September 21, Carla restored Leah and Kevin together, each recorded as "Cert confirmed (LMS re-pull 9/21)" (Exhibit 3).¹²⁵ Kevin, who had completed the course on September 11, was removed and restored on the same basis as Leah.¹²⁶ Carla's explanation of the weekly vendor sync accounts for both errors without reference to either representative's activity.¹²⁷

The strongest evidence against this conclusion is the timing and the state of the LMS at the time of removal. Marcus removed Leah at 7:05 AM on September 16, the morning after her complaint, and the policy directs that "The timing of an adverse action in relation to protected activity will be considered." Leah had completed the certification on August 28, and the LMS showed her completion from September 11, five days before Marcus acted on the September 10 list (Exhibit 3).¹²⁸ Marcus also knew of the accommodation request when he acted, because she sent it to him directly (Exhibit 2).¹²⁹ His statement that he first learned of the HR complaint on September 17 has no documentary corroboration.¹³⁰ Carla's statement that she did not know of the complaint does not bear on what Marcus knew.¹³¹ Marcus also has a stake in the outcome of this allegation. That stake is not evidence that his account is untrue.

These points do not change the outcome. Close timing and an unverified account of knowledge would carry weight if Leah had been singled out. The record shows instead that Marcus applied one pre-existing list, compiled by Workforce Management before the complaint, uniformly to four representatives. Kevin's certification also appeared in the LMS on September 11, and he was removed on the same stale list, which indicates that the error was in the list rather than directed at Leah.¹³² Whether Marcus should have checked the LMS before acting on a six-day-old list is a separate question from whether he acted because of protected activity. The record does not connect the removal to either the request or the complaint.

For these reasons, there is not sufficient evidence from which to infer that, more likely than not, Marcus Doyle removed Leah Goldberg from the escalations queue because she requested a religious accommodation or because she complained to HR.
